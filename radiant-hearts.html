Empowering others to serve.

The Radiant Hearts Foundation is the Blue Coding family's social impact arm — a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that funds and supports vetted grassroots projects strengthening communities across Latin America and the Caribbean. NearshoreRCM operates under the same umbrella, in the same cities: the communities the foundation serves are the communities our teams live in.

BOX 2

From Blue Coding to Radiant Hearts

Why a staffing company runs a grant-giving foundation

Blue Coding started in the Dominican Republic in 2014 as a small nearshore staffing company and grew across Latin America — offices, acquisitions, an international team that today numbers around 200 collaborators across the region. As it grew, one question kept coming up: how do we give back to the communities where our people live?

The first answers were ad-hoc — donations, and a long-running partnership with Animales en Peligro, Inc. in the Dominican Republic. That partnership taught a lesson that shaped everything after it: small, well-placed support changes lives, but giving effectively takes structure. Large NGOs absorbed too much in overhead; small grassroots organizations often couldn't access funding at all; trusted local options were hard to find. People wanted to help and there was no clear path.

So in 2022 the family built one: the Radiant Hearts Foundation — created to find, vet, and fund high-impact local projects, and to open that same pathway to other companies that want to give back. The foundation doesn't replace local organizations; it strengthens them, on the belief that the people closest to a problem are usually best equipped to solve it.

BOX 6

Case files

Three funded projects — animal welfare, women's economic empowerment, early childhood education

Animales en Peligro, Inc.
Dominican Republic The challenge

An enormous stray-animal population, born into abandonment and at high risk of illness.

The project

Eight spay-and-neuter campaigns across three years, offering free services in low-income communities.

Results

Over 250 animals sterilized and vaccinated — an estimated 15,000–25,000+ animals prevented from being born into abandonment over five years.

El Taller de Yely
Venezuela The challenge

Limited work and economic-growth opportunities for women in Venezuela.

The project

A three-month vocational program equipping women with sewing and garment-production skills and supporting them toward entrepreneurship as seamstresses.

Results

19 graduates in the initial cohort, a second cohort in planning — and next, a workshop where graduates take their first steps as independent entrepreneurs.

Fundación Tarbiat
Dominican Republic The challenge

Insufficient classroom space in San Juan de la Maguana, leaving local children without consistent schooling options.

The project

Equipping classrooms in the Fundación Tarbiat schoolhouse to establish pre-K — and later elementary — access for local children.

Results

Three early-childhood levels established with 24 children enrolled, and Ministry of Education approval to offer first and second grade next school year.
